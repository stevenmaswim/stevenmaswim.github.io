KSJ booking platform

PostgreSQL (Supabase, Row-Level Security) · Deno edge functions · pg_cron · Resend

ksjswimming.comsource

The problem

KSJ Swimming grew to 300+ students and 100+ active clients, booked into lessons across several coaches and pools. Paper tracking could not keep up. Families needed to book and cancel on their own, staff needed their own view, and two families must never get the same slot.

What I built

The production web app the business runs on. Families book with a login code sent by email. Staff sign in with Google, limited to an allowlist. Reminders go out every hour on a schedule, and the whole platform runs at $0 a month in hosting.

Decisions

Access control inside the database

Instead of trusting the app to check permissions, Postgres enforces them: Row-Level Security on 9 tables and a 3-tier role model. The point is that a bug in the front end should not be able to show one family another family's data: the database refuses to return it.

Double-booking blocked by the schema

A family can book two kids at once, so overlap checks are keyed per student. Booking several slots is atomic: rows are locked in id order, so two bookings can never wait on each other in a cycle. Underneath both, a partial unique index on bookings (slot_id) where status = 'confirmed' makes the database itself refuse a second confirmed booking for the same slot, even if the application code had a bug. The platform has 70+ tests that run against a live database.

One business timezone, everywhere

Every render and every write is pinned to the business timezone, with DST-correct conversion. A customer email never lists a 4 PM lesson as 2 PM.

After launch

  • Shipped features from customer and staff feedback: raised a daily login-code cap from 40 to 200 when families could not book, added per-coach cancellation alerts, and built rain-out handling (with a dry run and undo) that keeps closures out of revenue.
  • A pre-launch audit found 0 Critical issues and one High (CSV formula injection in staff exports), which I fixed.
