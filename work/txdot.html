Bridge condition forecasting

Software Engineering Intern, ML & Data · Texas Department of Transportation · Jun - Aug 2026

Python · SQL · XGBoost · scikit-learn · Snowflake

The problem

Texas inspects its bridges on a schedule and records a condition rating each time. The question was how each bridge will deteriorate over the next 10 and 20 years, using 1.7M inspection records for about 85K structures stored in an entity-attribute-value schema.

What I built

An end-to-end ML pipeline: multi-join SQL extraction from the inspection schema, feature building, model training and evaluation. The output went into Snowflake so the team could query it with SQL. On my supervisor's feedback, I filtered out decommissioned bridges.

Decisions

Forecast the change, not the snapshot

I recast the task as forecasting how a bridge deteriorates over time, with the time gap as an input. That turned the records into 575K inspection-pair training examples. The split is by bridge ID (GroupShuffleSplit, enforced by a test), so no bridge appears in both training and test data.

Trust the zeros

12 of the 28 model inputs scored exactly 0.0 importance in all 4 models. The pipeline ran cleanly, but those inputs were wired to NULL placeholders. I reconciled 65 export headers against the schema, and load-rating factors then showed up as top-5 drivers for every target in the first model.

Results

  • 90-96% of 68,959 held-out forecasts within ±1 point, at 10 and 20 years.
  • At 20 years: 1.5-1.6× lower error than carrying the last rating forward, and 12-21% below a Markov baseline.
  • I reported the one place it lost: 10-year forecasts for culverts.
